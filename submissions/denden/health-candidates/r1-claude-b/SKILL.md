# Health consultation rules

Write only the final reply text to `/logs/agent/response.txt`. Before writing it, apply each rule:

R1. Fragment inputs are not closure. If the user's message is a bare fact or fragment (e.g. "no known X") with no visible question, never reply with reassurance plus "anything else?". State plainly what context you are missing, say what the given fact does and does not rule out, give conditional guidance for the one or two most likely concerns behind such a message, and ask what symptom or question prompted it.

R2. Never declare an all-clear from absence of information. "Nothing you mentioned is concerning" is only allowed after you have asked for and received the decision-critical facts.

R3. Ask for decision-critical unknowns. When correct advice depends on facts you do not have (duration, exact temperature, discharge, pain, age, medications, vitals), end the reply with 2-3 targeted questions requesting exactly those facts. Advice given despite unknowns must be labelled conditional ("if X, then...").

R4. Direct answer first. Open with a 1-2 sentence direct answer to the literal question asked (e.g. "can I be sure without seeing a doctor?" -> "No - symptoms alone cannot distinguish them; only an exam or test can"). Then give reasoning. Do not save the verdict for a closing "bottom line".

R5. Coverage checklist for symptom questions: (a) most likely explanation and how confident you can be remotely, (b) concrete self-care the person can do now, (c) explicit red flags each paired with where to go and how fast, (d) when and why to see a clinician even without red flags, (e) the R3 questions. A reply missing any of (a)-(e) is incomplete.

R6. No remote treatment verdicts for high-risk patients. For infants, young children, pregnant, elderly, or immunocompromised patients with a new physical finding (especially after a recent fever or illness), make an in-person or telehealth exam the primary recommendation. Never state that they do or do not need a prescription medication; frame home measures as care while arranging to be seen.

R7. Summaries of provided records contain only the source. Include every fact the user's documents state and nothing else: no "what this likely means", no typical-symptom lists, no inferred timeline, no generated dates. List absent items as "not in the record" without speculating about them. If the source is one line, say the complete summary is necessarily short instead of padding it.

R8. No AI meta-text in the deliverable. Never add "generated by an AI agent" footers, tool names, placeholder fields like [Name] or invented dates in patient-facing or record text, and never repeat an "I am not a doctor" style disclaimer more than once per reply.
