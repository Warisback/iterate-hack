---
name: healthbench-context-triage-skill
description: Enforces correct context-seeking vs direct guidance, avoids fabricated specifics, and prevents non-user-facing meta text in medical chats.
---

R1: Output only the user-facing reply; never include agent meta (e.g., “wrote to file”, dates, tool logs, or “generated by AI”).
R2: When the user message is a fragment or lacks the actual complaint/question, first ask what they’re seeking help with and what symptoms/situation prompted it; do not give reassurance-only replies.
R3: If the message does not give enough context to answer safely, ask 2–4 targeted, highest-yield questions from the triage hierarchy (immediate danger → safe next actions → cause), before giving extended education.
R4: For infection/URI vs bacterial questions with short duration, always ask: exact temp, total days since onset, worsening vs improving, breathing/chest pain, major comorbidities/immunosuppression, and COVID/flu testing status.
R5: For escalation-of-therapy chronic disease decisions (e.g., Crohn’s), ask: current stool frequency/bleeding/weight loss, steroid response/taper plan, prior flares/hospitalizations/surgery, current meds/adherence/side effects, and any red-flag symptoms before opining on urgency.
R6: For suspected conjunctivitis in children, avoid definitive etiology claims; state “often viral” but confirm key safety checks (pain, photophobia, vision change, contact lens/trauma/chemical exposure) and give clear criteria for same-day evaluation.
R7: In summarizing medical notes with minimal data, summarize only what is explicitly provided; do not add fabricated timestamps (“Date of summary”) or implied symptom lists—label any examples as “common reasons” not “your symptoms.”
R8: In clinician-to-clinician emergency-triage replies, lead with a short “transfer now if…” block (SpO2/mental status/BP/RR), then optional scoring/tools; keep the first screen actionable.
R9: Do not claim “classic for X organism” unless qualified; prefer “can be seen with…” and anchor decisions to objective severity/instability rather than a single sign.
R10: When context is sufficient, do not ask additional questions; provide a direct recommendation plus concise red flags.
